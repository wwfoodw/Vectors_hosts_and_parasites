
# Vector Ecology

## Overview

Disease spread under climate change poses significant economic, environmental, and social risks. Climate change is driving the geographic redistribution of hosts and vectors of infectious microbes worldwide. While most vector-borne diseases—and the arthropods that transmit them—are currently considered absent from Aotearoa New Zealand, there is limited baseline knowledge of the infectious agents present in New Zealand's wildlife.

These knowledge gaps make it difficult to rapidly determine whether newly detected microbes are benign, beneficial, or pathogenic, reducing our ability to respond effectively to emerging disease threats.

## Repository Contents

This repository contains the metadata and code used in our studies of:

- Rangitāhua mosquito ecology study. Rangitāhua is an island archipelago located approximately 1000 km northeast of mainland Aotearoa New Zealand, that has had limited prior monitoring of mosquito populations and no assessment of the avian parasites they may carry. Included is a metadata file (rangitāhu_metadata.csv), bioinformatic script (rangitāhu_avian_malaria.sh) and R-code (rangitāhu_avian_malaria.R) required to reproduce the analyses in a pending publication looking at avian *Plasmodium* present in birds and mosquitoes on Rangitāhua as well as mosquito diet. The publication also reports the first observations that the globally problematic Southern house mosquito *Culex quinquefasciatus* has invaded the island.

The resources provided here are intended to support reproducible research in vector ecology, disease ecology, and biodiversity monitoring.

## Research Context

Our work focuses on improving understanding of vector-borne disease risks in Aotearoa New Zealand by generating baseline ecological and microbiological data. These data help inform biosecurity, public health preparedness, and responses to future climate-driven changes in disease distributions.
